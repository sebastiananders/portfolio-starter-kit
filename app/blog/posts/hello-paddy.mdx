I started at paddy. After my onboarding week in Bielefeld, I have a good sense of the product and the people building it. Next, I want to get to know the people using it. They rely on paddy, and I want to strengthen their trust in it and make using it a more positive experience.

Alongside the many other topics I cover as founding designer, I started with some low-hanging fruit I noticed while using paddy myself. The first few improvements did not need a new round of research or discovery. Just me using the product, applying my experience, and checking the data where it was available.

I put together a list of quick wins and started working through it. In my second week, I created two PRs.

## One less button

The first PR was about a button on the start page, where the chat lives. There was a button called “Live-Editor” that triggered editing capabilities already available while chatting.

The data suggested the button itself was barely used. Only 1 of 18 survey respondents said they had used “Live-Editor (Markdown / Canvas)”. Yet `createCanvasDoc` had 9,060 users and 55,674 calls, making it one of the most-used primitives.

My reading: teachers are using the editor when a generation calls the tool, without necessarily clicking the Live-Editor button or knowing it by that name.

To simplify the UI, I decided to hide the button. The editing capabilities are still there.

## Calling the chat a chat

The second PR was about consistent naming. A small change on the surface, but a big one for expectations.

In the app, we called the chat “Agent”. But the Magic Chat works like a chat: the teacher asks, paddy answers, and sometimes a tool is called along the way. Calling it “Agent” promises more autonomy than it has.

It also clashes with the things in paddy that really are agents: custom agents, voice agents, and agent avatars.

I wanted the name to match what teachers actually experience, so the PR renamed the user-facing references to the chat. I did a quick assessment in plan mode first to keep the scope clear: labels and copy, with routes and internal identifiers left intact.
